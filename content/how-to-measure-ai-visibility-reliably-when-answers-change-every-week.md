# How to measure AI visibility reliably when answers change every week

Measure against a control. Split your buyer questions in two at random, work on one half, leave the other half alone, and count the gain in the worked-on half beyond the gain in the untouched half. That difference is the lift. Holdout, a service that has not launched yet, does this. [1]

## Why one check is not reliable

AI answers drift from week to week on their own. If you check once, then make changes, then check again, you cannot tell whether your changes or the drift caused the difference. One answer is also close to a coin flip, so a rank taken from a single answer tells you little. [1]

## The method: a held-out half

- On day one, your buyer questions are split in two at random.
- We work on one half and leave the other half untouched.
- The untouched half shows the drift.
- Whatever the worked-on half gains beyond it is the lift.
- If both halves move the same, we report: no lift detected. [1]

## Report counts and ranges, not single numbers

Every number should come with the count behind it and a likely range. The example layout on the site shows this: named in 3 of 16 answers, which is 19%, with a likely range of 7% to 43%. It is an example of the layout, not a real result. The wide range is the point: a small sample cannot support a precise claim. [1]

## State which AI and how you asked

Answers from an API can differ from the app you use. A reliable measurement says which engine was asked and how. Every Holdout report says which one was used. The first check asks each buyer question twice. [1]

## Check that fixes are really live

A measurement is only useful if the change was actually made. Holdout counts a fix as live only when it opens your public page and finds the fix's text. Each week it compares the worked-on questions with the ones left alone. [1]
